## Q1. What outcome should Feature 079 optimize for?

The system should move beyond being a strong assistant and become a platform that can absorb real routine work across professions. The core product bar is not more chat or more tools. The bar is turning messy real-world work into reusable automation assets that can run with minimal human involvement.

## Q2. What level of human involvement is acceptable?

Human in the loop should be as low as possible. Human involvement should remain only at exception boundaries, policy-sensitive steps, irreversible actions, new grants, drift, or regulated situations. Happy-path routine work should not require repeated human confirmations.

## Q3. What kind of work should the first rollout target?

The first rollout should focus on routine operational work that is repetitive, high-volume, and lower-risk. Earlier discussion explicitly pointed toward finance operations, HR operations, support, sales operations, procurement, customer success, operations, and executive support rather than highly regulated or irreversible domains first.

## Q4. How should this feature relate to the broader AI organization vision?

Feature 079 should provide the reusable execution unit that future persistent role agents can own. It should not itself become the full persistent AI organization layer. That role belongs to Feature 080. Feature 079 must stop at the workpack layer and provide the safe substrate for future recurring role ownership.

## Q5. What constraints matter most for implementation?

The implementation should reuse the existing system rather than creating a parallel product. It should preserve current chat, workflow, browser, agency, and desktop surfaces, keep trust and audit strong, and use simulation, replay, and benchmark evidence before autonomous promotion.

## Q6. What makes the feature "done" from a product standpoint?

The feature is successful when a user can take a messy source such as a SOP, case study, screenshot set, or workflow export, convert it into a structured playbook and workpack, simulate it, run it with exception-only human intervention, learn from outcomes, and publish stable workpacks as reusable benchmark packs.
