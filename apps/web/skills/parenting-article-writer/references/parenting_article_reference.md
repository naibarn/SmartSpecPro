# Parenting Article Reference Notes

Use these notes as a lightweight internal reference when shaping the article.

## Tone & clarity
- Be warm, practical, and non-judgmental.
- Avoid shaming language (e.g., “you should have…”).
- Prefer short paragraphs, bullets, and numbered steps.

## Safety constraints (health topics)
- Provide general education only; no diagnosis.
- Avoid medication dosing and brand recommendations.
- Encourage caregivers to consult a licensed clinician for personal concerns.
- Include a “Red flags / When to seek medical care” section for any health-related topic.

## References (optional)
If show_references="yes":
- Provide 3–8 reputable sources (e.g., major children's hospitals, public health agencies, pediatric associations).
- Use short citations like: "AAP – Fever in children (aap.org)".
- Do not fabricate journal article titles; if unsure, keep references generic and reputable by domain/source type.

## Common “red flags” examples (general, not exhaustive)
Include only what is relevant to the topic and age range.
- Trouble breathing, blue/gray lips, severe lethargy, seizures
- Signs of dehydration (very few wet diapers/urination, very dry mouth, no tears, sunken soft spot in infants)
- Fever in very young infants or any fever with concerning symptoms
- Persistent vomiting, blood in stool/vomit, severe abdominal pain
- Rash with trouble breathing or swelling
- Head injury with worsening symptoms

Always recommend urgent care / emergency services when severe symptoms are present.

## Age-range tailoring reminders
- 0–3 months: emphasize feeding, hydration, breathing, safe sleep, and when to call a pediatrician urgently.
- 4–12 months: add solids guidance (safety), mobility safety, sleep routines.
- 1–3 years: tantrums, picky eating, language, toilet learning basics, injury prevention.
- 4–6 years: routines, social-emotional skills, school readiness, sleep hygiene.
