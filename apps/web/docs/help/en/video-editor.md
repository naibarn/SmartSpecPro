# Video Editor

## Overview

The Video Editor lets you assemble, trim, and export video content. It is designed for composing AI-generated clips alongside uploaded footage and audio.

## Getting to the editor

- Navigate to **Video Editor** in the sidebar.
- Open an existing video project or start a new one.
- Generated video clips from Chat appear in your media library and can be dragged into the timeline.

## Key capabilities

- **Timeline editing** — Arrange clips, images, and audio tracks on a multi-track timeline.
- **Trim and cut** — Resize clips by dragging the edges on the timeline.
- **AI-generated clips** — Pull in videos generated from Chat directly into your project.
- **Audio tracks** — Add background music or narration generated by the audio skill.
- **Export** — Render and download the final composition.

## Workflow

1. Generate video clips in Chat using `create video:` prompts.
2. Open the Video Editor and create a new project.
3. Drag your generated clips from the media library into the timeline.
4. Arrange and trim clips to build the sequence you want.
5. Add an audio track if needed.
6. Click **Export** to render the final video.

## Export options

- Click **Export** in the toolbar to render your video.
- Supported output formats: MP4 (H.264).
- Resolution follows your project settings (720p, 1080p).
- Export runs as a background task — you can continue editing while it renders.
- Download the finished file from the notification or the project page.

## Transitions and effects

- Use the **AI Draft** panel to generate transition suggestions between clips.
- Drag transitions from the panel onto the junction between two clips on the timeline.
- Supported transitions: fade, crossfade, slide, zoom.

## Tips

- Generate multiple clip variations in Chat before opening the editor — having options makes assembly faster.
- Use the AI Draft panel inside the editor for suggestions on transitions and sequencing.
- Keep individual clips short (5–15 seconds) for easier timeline management.
- Preview your edit frequently using the built-in player before exporting.
